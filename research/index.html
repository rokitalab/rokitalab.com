Integrative Multi-omics Approach

Our research harnesses the power of integrative multi-omics to unravel the complexities of pediatric brain tumors. By combining genomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics, we investigate how RNA splicing drives tumor formation and progression, and prioritize splice-derived peptides as candidate tumor-specific vulnerabilities for personalized immunotherapy. In collaboration with Dr. Sharon Diskin, we also characterize patient and parental germlines to pinpoint pathogenic variants that predispose patients to brain tumor development.

Clinical Trial Support

We bring real-time bioinformatics to the clinic, analyzing patient tumor data to help guide personalized treatment strategies for children with brain tumors.

IMPACT: Immunotherapy for Malignant Pediatric Brain Tumors Employing Adoptive Cellular Therapy (IMPACT) is a phase 1 interventional study led by Dr. Brian R. Rood. It tests personalized tumor-specific antigen T cells (TSA-T), which target proteogenomically determined antigens from each patient’s own tumor, in children under 5 years of age with embryonal brain tumors. As a sub-investigator, Dr. Rokita led a team of bioinformatics scientists and engineers in developing and implementing the IMPACT bioinformatics workflows, which are available on GitHub.

SUPERNOVA-RBT: SUPERNOVA-RBT is a pilot interventional study led by Dr. Adriana Fonseca that tests real-time drug screening and genomic sequencing of tumor tissue taken during surgery, in children and young adults with relapsed ultra-rare brain tumors. A specialized tumor board uses the results to assign a personalized treatment plan. As a sub-investigator, Dr. Rokita oversaw the bioinformatic integration of genomic, transcriptomic, and drug-screen data, which places each tumor’s response to therapy in its molecular context.

Health Disparities

We are deeply passionate about exploring the impact of social determinants of health, race, ethnicity, and genetic-based ancestry on pediatric brain tumors. In collaboration with Dr. Cassie Kline, we identified significant associations between genetic ancestry superpopulations, CNS tumor histologies and molecular subtypes, patient survival, and upfront treatment approaches.

Genetic ancestry associated disparities Neuro-Oncology, 2025

Distinct associations of genetic ancestry-based superpopulations exist within pediatric CNS tumor histologic and molecular subtypes and correlate with survival outcomes and treatment.
